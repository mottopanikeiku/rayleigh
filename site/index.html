Paired sample-size calculator

Plan a paired model comparison

Choose the smallest accuracy gap you care about, then say how often the models disagree: as an assumption, or as counts from a pilot. The calculator turns that into an item count using a two-sided Gaussian approximation.

Inputs

In a paired comparison both models answer the same items, while in an unpaired one each model gets a separate set of items.

Target difference

A 2-point gap means 70% versus 72%, and the baseline accuracy matters because it helps determine the variance.

Study design

Discordance counts items on which exactly one model is correct, and not every correlation is possible for two binary accuracies. With pilot counts, the plan uses a one-sided 95% upper bound on discordance, as the study recommends when models disagree on 5% of items or fewer.

Error rates

Alpha is the false-positive rate for one comparison, and power is the chance of detecting the target gap under your assumptions.

Each benchmark has 40 model pairs chosen independently of scores, with five pilot splits per pair. Each plan with a usable gap and variance gets 1,000 exact two-sided McNemar trials, sampling unused items with replacement, against a target of 80% power at alpha 0.05.

The data cover 395 models from the 2024 Open LLM Leaderboard. The results below describe those pilot plans, so they stay fixed when you change the calculator inputs.

Every plan and setting behind these numbers is in the repository's results folder.

Calculation

For a gap δ, the unpaired variance is pA(1 − pA) + pB(1 − pB). For paired items it is q − δ², where q is discordance, or equivalently the unpaired variance minus twice the covariance. From pilot counts, the variance v is the one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson upper bound on q, which also bounds q − δ². I find the smallest integer n with Φ(−z − |δ|√(n/v)) + Φ(|δ|√(n/v) − z) at least the target power, where z is the two-sided normal critical value.

The browser and Python planners agree within one item on the test grid, and the browser's discordance bounds match SciPy's within 10−12. With an assumed disagreement rate the Gaussian budget can fall short when disagreement is rare, and any Gaussian budget can fall short for very small samples. Clustered questions or several comparisons need their own adjustment.
